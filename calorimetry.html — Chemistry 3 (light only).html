Calorimetry

Measure the heat given out or taken in when a solid dissolves in water. Track the temperature before and after, extrapolate back to the moment of mixing, and work out the molar enthalpy change.

1 · Setup

Solution
Calorimeter (optional)

Use an insulated cup with a lid, stir gently and continuously, and use a thermometer that reads to 0.1 °C. Take the reagent your instructor approves — the presets are common, safe classroom salts.

2 · Run

Start the timer, and record the temperature every 15–20 s. Record at least three readings before mixing, press “Mark mixing” as you tip the solid in, then keep recording for a few minutes.

3 · Analyse

Sign conventions: a negative ΔH means the dissolving gives out heat (exothermic) and the mixture warms up; a positive ΔH means it takes in heat (endothermic) and the mixture cools. Literature values (KCl +17.2, NH₄Cl +14.8, NaCl +3.9 kJ mol⁻¹) are approximate — your result will be a few per cent out because the cup itself absorbs heat.
