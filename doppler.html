Doppler effect

One phone plays a steady tone; the other listens. Move the tone towards and away from the microphone and the page tracks the frequency shift, then converts it into speed.

1 · Setup

You need two phones (or one phone and a speaker). Phone A plays a pure tone; phone B runs this page as the listener. Use a quiet room — talking, fans and wind across the microphone all upset the measurement. Keep the tone between 300 and 5000 Hz; phone microphones are most sensitive there.

Speed of sound used: 343.4 m/s at this temperature.

2 · Emitter (phone A)

If phone A does not have a tone generator, this page can play the tone itself — but then use a different phone as the listener, or the sound leaks straight into its own microphone.

3 · Listener (phone B)

Prop this phone up, press Start listening, and check the frequency readout locks onto the tone (about 1000 Hz). Then start recording and move phone A steadily towards and away — or swing it on a string in a circle.

Export

The microphone needs HTTPS (GitHub Pages is fine) and permission from the browser. Moving source: f = f₀·c/(c−v) approaching, f₀·c/(c+v) receding. The page reports speeds from the largest frequency shift, so move smoothly and steadily — jerky motion smears the peak. Background noise is the number one cause of failure: close windows and keep still while recording.
