Projectile motion

Throw a brightly coloured ball across the camera's view. The page follows it through the air, fits the parabola, and measures gravity, the launch speed and the launch angle — straight from the video.

1 · Set it up

Prop the phone up so it sees the whole flight, and lay a ruler or a book of known length flat in the plane the ball will fly through — the scale comes from it. Use a plainly coloured ball (an orange or a tennis ball) against a plain wall or sky. Throw it gently at first; a big lob is easier to track than a fast throw.

3 · Set the scale

Press Calibrate, then tap the two ends of the ruler or book in the picture. Type its real length and press Use this scale. Keep the ruler in the plane of the throw, roughly where the ball flies.

4 · Analyse

Try it without a throw

The camera tracks the ball by colour at up to 30 frames a second. Horizontally the ball moves at a steady speed, so x(t) is fitted with a straight line; vertically gravity bends the path, so y(t) is fitted with a parabola. The parabola's curvature is g: with the scale in metres per pixel, g = 2×(curvature)×(scale). The launch speed and angle come from the two fitted lines at t = 0. The flight should be a clean arc: no bounces in the recording, and the ball visible for its whole flight. If tracking drops out mid-flight, throw more slowly or against a plainer background. Demo data shows a typical gentle lob: it recovers g to within about 1%. Needs the camera, so the page must be opened over HTTPS (GitHub Pages is fine).
