Reaction kinetics

Watch a coloured reaction fade, record its colour over time, and work out whether the reaction is zero, first or second order — and its rate constant.

1 · The reaction

Use an instructor-approved fading reaction — for example, dilute blue food dye with household bleach, with the volumes your teacher sets. Mix, start the timer at the same moment, and record a reading every 20–30 s until the colour has almost gone. Keep the cup, background, lighting and camera position fixed for the whole run.

Safety: bleach irritates skin and eyes; wear goggles, avoid inhaling fumes, and follow your instructor’s instructions.

Keep every run in the same light. The camera needs HTTPS (e.g. GitHub Pages) — if it will not start, take photos with the phone’s camera app and load them, or type RGB values. Rate constants come out in whatever concentration units the colour represents — compare orders between runs, not absolute k values across different setups.
