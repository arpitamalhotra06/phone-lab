Ramp: acceleration and friction

Let the phone slide down a slope. The page measures how fast it speeds up, then works out the friction between the phone and the ramp.

  1. Make a ramp from a smooth board or a thick book cover, at an angle steep enough for the phone to slide. Put a pillow at the bottom to stop the phone gently.
  2. Lay the phone flat on the ramp, screen up, held still by a finger.
  3. Tap Start, then Ready. Keep holding it. When the page says "Let go now", lift your finger and let it slide.

While the phone rests on the ramp, the page records the reading and finds which way is down the slope. When you let go, it measures how much the reading along the slope changes. That change is the acceleration a, and the phone must keep its orientation as it slides. The first and last 0.15 s of the slide are ignored, and the middle value (median) is used, so the release and the stop do not count. Without friction a = g sin θ. With friction a = g (sin θ − μ cos θ), so μ = tan θ − a / (g cos θ). Here g = 9.8 m/s². The phone must slide for at least about half a second. If it does not slide at all, the ramp is too shallow.
