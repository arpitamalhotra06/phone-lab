Earth's magnetic field

Measure the dip angle — how steeply Earth's magnetic field points into the ground at your campus — and the total field strength, then check both against the official IGRF model for your exact coordinates.

1 · Set it up

You need a flat surface and a wall corner. Move away from metal, magnets, laptops and wires — they bend the reading. The phone's magnetometer measures the field in three directions at once: across the screen, up the screen, and straight out of it.

2 · Measure the field

Live sensors (Chrome on Android): tap Start, hold the phone still in any comfortable orientation, and the page works out the dip angle itself using gravity as the reference.

4 · Compare with the model

The IGRF is the internationally agreed model of Earth's field, built from observatories and satellites. Enter your campus coordinates — or use your GPS — and the page computes what the model predicts at your spot, today.

The dip angle is how steeply the field points: 0° means horizontal (near the equator), 90° means straight down (at the magnetic poles). In the southern USA it is about 60°. The page computes it from the three magnetometer axes combined with the direction of gravity (from the accelerometer), so the phone can be held in any orientation. Field strength at Earth's surface is typically 25–65 µT. The comparison uses the 14th generation IGRF (2025 coefficients with secular variation), the same model behind official calculators — evaluated on your phone, offline. Live sensors need Chrome on Android and a secure (https) page. iPhones do not expose raw magnetometer readings to web pages, so use the typed-values route there.
