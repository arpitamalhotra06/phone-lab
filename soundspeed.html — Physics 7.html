Speed of sound

Play a steady tone out loud and slide the phone towards a wall. The direct sound and the echo interfere, so the loudness rises and falls — and the distance between loud points reveals the speed of sound.

1 · Set it up

Find a bare wall and grab a ruler or tape measure. Face the phone’s speaker towards the wall, holding it flat and level, parallel to the wall. The mic and speaker stay on the same phone — you only need one phone. Keep the volume at about half.

3 · Take readings

Start with the phone about 5 cm from the wall. Type the distance, tap Take reading, then slide the phone 3 cm further and repeat. Keep going out to about 50–60 cm — aim for at least 12 readings. Hold the phone still for a moment at each distance.

4 · Fit the wave

The amplitude should swing up and down as you moved — one full cycle of loud–quiet–loud every half wavelength. The page fits the best sine wave to your readings and works out the wavelength, then the speed of sound from v = fλ.

The speaker plays a steady tone while the microphone listens. The direct sound and the sound reflected from the wall arrive slightly out of step, and their phase difference changes as you move — so the recorded loudness follows a sine wave in distance. Adjacent maxima are half a wavelength apart, and v = fλ. The fit searches speeds from 300 to 400 m/s and finds the one whose sine wave matches your readings best (least squares). Test it on the demo data first: it recovers about 343 m/s. Needs the microphone and speaker at once, so use headphones-free speakers, not a headset. Works best in a quiet room with a hard wall.
