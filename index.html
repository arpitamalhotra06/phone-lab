Phone Lab

Pick a subject, then an experiment.

Biology

1Specimen labBiology

For teacher-supervised labs. Pick an animal, study the organs, explore a photo of a specimen, and take a quiz.
2Animal behaviourBiology

Put pillbugs or fruit flies between two conditions, like moist and dry. The page times the trial, counts each side with tap counters, and tests whether the preference is statistically real.

Physics

1Drop heightPhysics

Drop the phone onto carpet or a bed. The page times the fall and works out how far it dropped.
2Pendulum periodPhysics

Point the phone camera at a swinging pendulum. The page tracks the bob and measures the time of one full swing, T.
3Tilt anglePhysics

Rest the phone on a slope or a book. The page measures the angle from gravity, and you check it with a protractor.
4Ramp and frictionPhysics

Let the phone slide down a slope. The page measures the acceleration and works out the friction.

Chemistry

1ColourimeterChemistry

Photograph coloured standards with the phone camera, build a Beer–Lambert calibration line from absorbance, and read off the concentration of an unknown sample.
2Reaction kineticsChemistry

Watch a coloured reaction fade and record its colour over time. The page fits zero-, first- and second-order models, picks the best one, and gives the rate constant and half-life.
3CalorimetryChemistry

Track the temperature of a dissolving solid before and after mixing. The page extrapolates the change at the moment of mixing and works out the molar enthalpy change.
Open this page on your phone. Physics experiments need motion sensors and work best in Safari (iPhone) or Chrome (Android). The biology and chemistry labs use the camera and touchscreen — the chemistry labs also accept typed values, so they work without a camera. Chemistry experiments should use instructor-approved reactions only.
