Doppler effect

One phone plays a steady tone; the other listens. Move either one towards and away from the other to see the pitch shift and signed speed.

1 · Setup

You need two phones (or one phone and a speaker). Phone A plays a pure tone; phone B runs this page as the listener. Use a quiet room — talking, fans and wind across the microphone all upset the measurement. Keep the tone between 300 and 5000 Hz; phone microphones are most sensitive there.

Frequency range and time step

Shorter steps follow faster changes. Longer steps average over more audio and need a narrower range.

Speed of sound used: 343.4 m/s at this temperature.

2 · Emitter (phone A)

If phone A does not have a tone generator, this page can play the tone itself — but then use a different phone as the listener, or the sound leaks straight into its own microphone.

3 · Listener (phone B)

Keep one phone still and select which phone will move. Press Start recording, then move that phone smoothly towards and away from the other. If calibration is on, keep both still until the app says to move.

4 · Analyse

Only enter a string length for full circular rotations. Leave it blank for a back-and-forth arm swing.

Recorded speed: + getting closer, − getting farther apart.

Speed at a chosen moment

For mid-swing, film from the side and capture the Start recording tap. Enter the time from that tap to the halfway point in your video, or tap the graph. The microphone cannot identify halfway by itself.

For the forearm demo setup, swing the tone source and keep the listening phone roughly level with your elbow, in the plane of the swing and several arm lengths away.

Export

The microphone needs HTTPS (GitHub Pages is fine) and browser permission. Keep one device still. Doppler measures how fast the distance between the two devices changes, not the total speed along a curved path.
