Bouncing ball energy loss

Use the time between bounce sounds to estimate how high the ball rebounds and how much energy it loses.

Record the bounces

Put your phone on a table beside a hard floor in a quiet room. Tap Record bounces and allow microphone access. Wait for Drop the ball now, then release a bouncy ball without pushing it. Keep the phone out of the ball’s path and stay quiet until the bounces finish.

Recording lasts up to ten seconds. No audio is uploaded.

These are candidate impacts. Untick taps, footsteps or obvious duplicates. If quiet bounces were missed, try raising the sensitivity.

Enter impact times manually instead

Paste times in seconds from a recording, separated by commas, spaces or new lines. Start with the first impact, not the release.

Rebound height and energy

At least two impacts give one rebound height. At least three give an energy loss estimate.

Rebound afterFlight (s)Height (cm)Energy loss

We estimate height as g × flight time² ÷ 8. We estimate the loss at the next impact by comparing consecutive rebound heights. This measures the ball’s vertical mechanical energy under a simple model, not an exact total energy loss.
