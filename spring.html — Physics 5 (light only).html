Springs and Hooke’s law

Find a spring’s stiffness two ways: by stretching it with known masses, and by letting the phone bounce on it and timing the oscillation. Then check that the two answers agree.

1 · Setup

Hang a spring (or a stack of rubber bands) from a hook or shelf edge. Weigh the phone on a kitchen scale first and note its mass. For the static part you need a ruler and a few known masses (coins or weights). For the bouncing part, hold the phone in a loop of string, or in a small bag, on the spring — keep the bounce small and vertical, and stop it swinging sideways.

2 · Static: Hooke’s law

Add masses one at a time and measure the length of the spring each time (include a row with 0 g for the unloaded length). Hooke’s law says F = k x, so force against extension should be a straight line with gradient k.

3 · Dynamic: bouncing phone

Hang the phone from the spring, pull it down a couple of centimetres, let go, then press Start. The accelerometer picks up the bounce and the page measures the period T. Repeat with extra mass taped to the phone each time.

Dynamic method: for a mass m on a spring, T² = 4π²(m + ms/3)/k, where ms is the spring’s own mass. A line of T² against m has gradient 4π²/k, and its intercept tells you the spring’s effective mass. Keep the extension within the spring’s elastic range, or the line will curve.
